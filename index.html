About

From jobsites to circuit boards

I grew up working construction with my family's company, TE Taylor Co., coordinating crews on commercial concrete and ADA-compliant sitework long before I set foot in an engineering classroom. That hands-on foundation is still how I approach every project — understand how it's actually built, then make it better.

Today I'm an Electrical Engineering student at USF, concentrating in power systems, serving as Project Lead for IEEE EXO's robotic gait-assist leg system — where I also lead Mime, its flagship gesture-controlled robotic hand — and doing undergraduate research at the TROPICS Lab. Before that, I spent a year and a half as an Assembly Technician on UL-certified lithium-ion battery systems at Lithionics. On the side I build software for people I know, like a volleyball coaching app that's seen a team through a state championship season, and I'm slowly restoring a century-old Sears catalog house with my dad.

IEEE EXO Flagship Project

Mime — Gesture-Controlled Robotic Hand

A gesture-controlled 5-finger tendon-driven robotic hand with two input modes: a webcam computer-vision pipeline (MediaPipe hand-tracking) and a flex-sensor glove. Built on an ESP32 with a PCA9685 servo driver, with calibration tooling and a debounced gesture-command interface for reliable real-time actuation.

Materials Science / Electrochemistry

Organic Electroplating — "Copper Frog"

Electroplated a copper coating onto an organic specimen using a conductive graphite-painted cathode in a copper sulfate electrolytic cell with a copper-wire anode and a 6V DC source, depositing over 10g of copper in 6 hours. Applied Faraday's laws of electrolysis to relate deposited mass to charge and current.

In Progress

301/501 Darts Scorer — STM32 Redesign

A 1–4 player electronic darts scorer for 301 or 501, singles or 2v2 teams — originally an Arduino/ATmega328P 2-player prototype, now redesigned around an STM32F103C8T6 for the extra headroom. Dual I2C-driven 7-segment displays, a 16x2 LCD, RGB status LED, and buzzer/speaker callouts, with a 4x4 matrix keypad replacing the original IR remote. Developed in Cursor against a CubeMX-generated CMake project, built with ARM GCC, and flashed/debugged over ST-Link and OpenOCD.

MCU STM32F103C8T6 (Blue Pill) Toolchain CubeMX, Cursor, ARM GCC Status Breadboard prototyping
